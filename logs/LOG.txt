	■ attempting to polyphonicize <MacroPerc copy 3 M.vcv> - checking out what exactly the CV input mapping is for macro oscillator's knobs. turns out they all use 0-8V for some reason. will save patch with notes!
	■ argued with this thing for a long time - problem is, the macro oscillator 2 only has one CV attenuverter per function, and you can't send it a polyphonic signal. So if I'm going to replace Bill's six Macro Oscillators 2 with a single polyphonic one, I need to figure out how to recreate their various CV attenuations with oscillations attenuated before they get to the module, and summed with the base voltage. The problem is that the attenuation curve of the CV attenuverter is NOT LINEAR. WHY NOT.
	
	And. The maximum voltage it can understand is 8.02 volts exactly. If you open the attenuverter all the way ("1") and send 8.02V to the Timbre CV, you hear the highest timbre setting the module can produce. But. If you set the attenuator to half open ("0.5") and send it 8.02V, this is equivalent to  having it open all the way and sending it 2V - a quarter the value. it's kind of like......
			f(x/y) = x/(y^2)
	
	which is almost certainly a pretty simple peice of trigonometry or something, I just need to figure out how to spell it out with math VCV rack can understand. It persists, too - opening the attenuverter 1/4 of the way ("0.25") and passing it 8.02V is equivalent to an open-attenuverter input of 0.5V - 1/16 of 8V! (4^2=16)
